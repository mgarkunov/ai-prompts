# Digital Lead Generation Strategy Prompt

A starting prompt for creating a final brief to develop a digital strategy for a micro or small business. Here, digital means digital acquisition channels and the associated journey from first contact to sale. This template does not develop the strategy itself: it helps collect inputs, agree on the scope, and prepare a self-contained final prompt.

## Context fields

- **Business and offering** — industry or niche, products or services, business stage, sales model, team size, and delivery capacity. Do not treat micro and small businesses as a single audience: specify who buys from this business separately.
- **Market and geography** — country, region or service area, language, B2B or B2C, local or online model, analysis period, and seasonality if known.
- **Customers and needs** — known segments, buying situations, needs, objections, decision-makers, and evidence for these inputs.
- **Objective and inquiry quality** — the required outcome and deadline, definitions of a lead, an inquiry, and a qualified inquiry; what counts as a suitable customer, a duplicate, or spam.
- **Business economics** — average sale value, margin or contribution toward covering costs, repeat purchases, sales cycle, and existing acquisition cost limits. Leave unknown values unknown.
- **Budget and resources** — separate spending on traffic, assets, tools, and labor; the owner's available time, team roles, and capacity to engage contractors.
- **Current situation** — website, landing pages, profiles, advertising, content, reputation, active channels, and results for a stated period.
- **Inquiry handling** — how inquiries arrive, the responsible person, working hours, response time, sales stages, reasons for lost opportunities, and the current CRM or other tracking method.
- **Data and sources** — anonymized statistics, sales results, research, reviews, available sources, and restrictions on web research.
- **Constraints and preferences** — prohibited channels, data requirements, jurisdiction, existing tools, required integrations, and reasons for preferences.
- **Time horizon and format** — strategy period, pilot timeline, intended audience, approval owner, language, and required level of detail.

## Sections to consider before the final prompt

In addition to market analysis, channel selection, and inquiry management tools, discuss:

- customer segmentation, positioning, and the offer — whom to target, what to offer, and why;
- the customer journey and conversion points — where to send traffic and what action to expect;
- acquisition economics and budget — which costs the business can recover;
- inquiry handling and sales — who turns an inquiry into a sale;
- analytics and lead quality — how to distinguish more inquiries from more valuable outcomes;
- the pilot, stopping criteria, and scaling criteria — how to test hypotheses;
- resources, risks, and constraints — what this business can realistically execute.

Include repeat purchases, customer referrals, partnerships, reputation, and automation where they support the objective. Do not expand the strategy to cover every marketing task without approval.

Copy the text below and fill in the fields in square brackets. If data is unavailable, write "unknown." Do not provide customers' personal data, passwords, or access keys.

```text
Create a ready-to-use prompt for developing a digital strategy to generate leads and customer inquiries for a micro or small business.

Context:
- Business and offering: [niche, products or services, stage, sales model, team, delivery capacity]
- Market and geography: [country, service area, language, B2B or B2C, local or online, period, seasonality]
- Customers and needs: [segments, buying situations, needs, objections, evidence]
- Objective and inquiry quality: [outcome, deadline, lead and inquiry definitions, qualification criteria]
- Business economics: [average sale value, margin, repeat purchases, sales cycle, acquisition cost limits]
- Budget and resources: [traffic, assets, tools, labor, owner's time, roles, contractors]
- Current situation: [platforms, channels, assets, reputation, results and period]
- Inquiry handling: [inquiry sources, responsible person, working hours and response time, stages, losses, tracking]
- Data and sources: [anonymized materials, research, sources, access to web research]
- Constraints and preferences: [prohibitions, jurisdiction, data requirements, tools, integrations]
- Time horizon and format: [strategy period, pilot, audience, approval owner, language, detail]

Work in sequence:
1. Assess the inputs. If the business and offering, geography, target customers, objective, or resource limits cannot be established, ask no more than five prioritized clarifying questions and stop. Do not create the final prompt until answers are provided. Do not replace an explicit "unknown" with an invented value: state which information must be gathered while developing the strategy and which decisions cannot yet be made.
2. Before creating the final prompt, propose a concise strategy outline. Include market analysis, channel selection, and inquiry management tools. Propose relevant additional sections: audience and offer, customer journey, economics, inquiry handling, analytics, pilot, resources, and risks. Explain which decision each additional section supports; exclude irrelevant sections. Wait for approval of the scope unless the user has already approved it in the current discussion.
3. After approval, return only one self-contained final prompt in English in a code block, with no explanation before or after it. Include the confirmed context, boundaries, unknowns, and selected sections; do not rely on earlier messages as the sole source of the brief.

The prompt you create must specify the following requirements, combining related items into the agreed structure without repetition:

1. Role and objective
   The role of a practical digital strategist for micro and small businesses. The objective is an evidence-based strategy for generating qualified inquiries and converting them into sales, proportionate to the business's budget and capacity. Specify the audience, decision owner, time horizon, boundaries, and non-goals. Do not promise a guaranteed number of inquiries or sales.

2. Diagnosis and definitions
   Assess current platforms, channels, analytics, tracking, and inquiry handling using available data. Distinguish a visit, a lead, an inquiry, a qualified inquiry, and a sale; agree on qualification criteria and how to account for duplicates and spam. List baseline metrics, missing data, and checks required before spending the budget.

3. Market analysis for a micro or small business
   Define the relevant market for this specific business: niche, geography, demand, seasonality, competitors, substitutes, offerings, prices, and acquisition approaches. Do not replace this analysis with general small-business statistics. If the buyers are themselves micro or small businesses, segment them separately by industry, size, maturity, need, and buying process. Separate observable competitor activity from unavailable evidence about their spending and performance; do not treat a channel's popularity as proof of profitability.

4. Audience, positioning, and offer
   Identify priority segments, customer needs and tasks, demand triggers, objections, reasons for choosing the business, and alternatives. Propose an offer, trust signals, messaging, and a call to action for the selected segments. Treat unverified audience assumptions as hypotheses with a validation method; do not invent reviews, case studies, or advantages.

5. Channel discovery and selection
   Consider relevant channel categories: search engine optimization, search and other paid advertising, maps and local directories, social media, content, industry platforms, partnerships, and referrals. Separate repeat communication with existing contacts from new customer acquisition. Select specific platforms according to geography, audience behavior, availability, and constraints; do not include every channel by default.
   Compare candidate channels by customer segment and intent, role in the path to a sale, offer, traffic destination, required assets, spending and labor, testing timeline, risks, inquiry quality, and measurement method. Justify priorities without invented scores. Select the smallest sufficient set for a pilot; separately explain what is deferred or excluded and why.

6. Customer journey and conversion
   Describe the journey from exposure to a channel through inquiry, qualification, and sale. Review relevant conversion points: website or landing page, business listing, form, phone call, booking, or messaging app. Define the required offer, information, trust signals, and customer's next step. Do not recommend a new website, a complex funnel, or a suite of services if existing tools meet the need.

7. Economics and budget
   Separate the budget into media spending, asset production, setup, tools, and ongoing labor. Calculate only metrics supported by inputs; state formulas, included costs, units, period, and assumptions. Distinguish cost per lead (CPL), cost per qualified inquiry, and customer acquisition cost (CAC). Do not present average sale value as profit, cost per click as cost per inquiry, or repeat purchases as guaranteed income.
   Assess acceptable acquisition cost using the business's economics and the owner's requirements. Do not impose universal profitability thresholds. If data is missing, propose how to collect it; clearly label scenarios and ranges as calculations, not forecasts of results. Account for the team's capacity to handle inquiries and fulfill sales.

8. Lead and inquiry management and CRM
   Describe a minimal process: inquiry registration, source, responsible person, first response, qualification, next step, deal stage, reminder, and reason for loss. Choose response times and automation scope based on working hours and team capacity, not an invented standard.
   First define requirements for customer relationship management (CRM) or simple tracking. Consider Bitrix24 as one candidate, not a mandatory solution. Compare it with suitable alternatives and a simpler option on inquiry tracking, forms, telephony or messaging, integrations, reporting, access permissions, data export, implementation complexity, and total cost of ownership.
   Verify current pricing, limits, country availability, and features of the required edition using official sources, recording the verification date. Include licenses, setup, integrations, training, and support. Justify the recommendation and the conditions that would change it; do not select a specific system if critical requirements are unknown. Do not connect services or upload real customer data while developing the strategy.

9. Analytics and inquiry quality
   Define how to link a traffic source to an inquiry and a sale: relevant UTM parameters, events, identifiers, CRM, and call tracking only where necessary. Specify metrics, data sources, the responsible person, reporting period, and inquiry quality criteria. Link channel evaluation to qualification, sales, and economics, not only reach and clicks.
   State attribution limits, repeat inquiries, the delay between inquiry and sale, incomplete data, and duplicates. Do not treat the sum of metrics from different advertising platforms as a count of unique customers or present attribution as proof of a causal effect.

10. Pilot and implementation plan
    For each selected channel, define the hypothesis, segment, offer, assets, resource limit, duration accounting for the sales cycle, measurement method, and criteria for continuing, adjusting, or stopping. Use thresholds from confirmed conditions or justify them through calculations; obtain the owner's approval for proposed thresholds. Do not conclude success from a single inquiry or an observation period that is too short.
    Prepare a sequenced plan: establish tracking and inquiry handling, launch a limited pilot, review quality and sales, then decide whether to scale. For each task, specify the output, executor's role, deadline, dependency, and verification method. Do not assign people or dates without evidence. Propose automation for an established process, not as a substitute for defining one.

11. Risks and action boundaries
    Address relevant risks involving platforms, demand, budget, reputation, data access, dependence on contractors, and team overload. Verify advertising, personal data, and messaging requirements for the stated jurisdiction; do not present an unverified statement as a legal conclusion. Do not propose spam, unlawfully obtained contact lists, or bypassing consent.
    Analysis and recommendations do not authorize launching advertising, spending money, registering accounts, installing integrations, publishing, or sending messages. These actions require separate explicit permission.

12. Sources and unknowns
    For changing external information about markets, platforms, tools, and rules, verify current sources online if access is available and the task allows it. Prefer primary data and transparent methods for statistics, and official documentation for features and pricing. Give a direct link, organization, date, and scope of applicability alongside each material fact. Check material comparative or causal conclusions against independent evidence; explicitly state reliance on a single source or a marketing claim as a limitation.
    Distinguish confirmed facts, business data, conclusions, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations. Do not invent demand, prices, budgets, conversion rates, sources, or research findings. Without access to verification, disclose the limitation and required checks; do not present unverified information as current. Use only anonymized data, do not request secrets, and do not disclose confidential materials.

13. Output format and acceptance
    Require a concise summary of priorities and decisions, the agreed strategy sections, channel comparison, inquiry handling workflow and tool selection, economics and budget, pilot plan, metrics and checkpoints, sources, unknowns, and the next safe step.
    Use tables for comparisons and plans; do not repeat the same information across sections. Write clearly for a small-business owner and explain necessary terms. Check strategy readiness against the connection between segment, offer, channel, inquiry point, handling, sale, and measurement; compliance with resource limits; verifiable recommendations; and explicit conditions for decisions. Identify critical gaps as limitations on readiness rather than hiding them behind a confident conclusion.

Do not develop the strategy itself, conduct market research, or select tools at this stage. Create only the final prompt after the necessary clarifications and approval of the structure.
```
